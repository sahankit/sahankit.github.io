EMI formula

Banks in India use the reducing-balance formula: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly instalments.

Example: ₹5,00,000 at 10.5% for 5 years (60 months) gives r = 0.00875 and an EMI of about ₹10,747. You repay about ₹6,44,800 in total, so the interest cost is roughly ₹1,44,800.

How to lower your EMI or total interest

  • A longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases total interest paid.
  • Even a 0.5% lower rate saves a lot on long home loans—compare lenders.
  • Part-prepayments early in the loan cut interest the most, because early EMIs are mostly interest.
  • A larger down payment reduces the principal and every EMI after it.

Reading the repayment schedule

The year-wise table shows how each year's EMIs split between principal and interest and the balance left at year end. In the first years most of the EMI goes to interest; the principal share grows over time.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official bank quote?

No. It is an estimate using the standard formula. Processing fees, insurance and rate resets on floating loans are not included.

Does the EMI change with a floating rate?

Yes. When the rate changes, banks either change the EMI or extend the tenure. Re-run the calculator with the new rate to estimate it.

Can I use this for home, car and personal loans?

Yes. The formula is the same for any reducing-balance loan. Only the rate and tenure differ.

What is included in total payment?

Total payment is EMI × number of months, which equals the principal plus all interest over the tenure.

Is this EMI calculator free?

Yes. The EMI calculator is free to use in your browser with no account required.
