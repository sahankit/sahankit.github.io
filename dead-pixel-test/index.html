Dead pixels vs stuck pixels

  • Dead pixel — always black, most visible on the white screen. Usually permanent.
  • Stuck pixel — stays red, green, blue or white; most visible on black or contrasting colours. Sometimes recovers on its own.
  • Backlight bleed — light leaking at the edges on the black screen in a dark room.
  • Dust — a spot that moves or disappears when you wipe the screen isn’t a pixel fault.

How to test a new monitor or laptop

Wipe the screen with a microfibre cloth, set brightness high and start the test. Look at each colour from a normal viewing distance, then up close, scanning section by section. Do it within your return or warranty window—manufacturers’ dead-pixel policies often require a minimum number of faulty pixels.

Frequently asked questions

Does it work on phones and TVs?

Yes. Open the page on the device and tap to change colours. Smart-TV browsers may not allow true full screen.

Can a stuck pixel be fixed?

Sometimes. Gently massaging the spot with a soft cloth while the screen is on, or leaving the display off for a while, can help. Dead pixels usually can’t be fixed.

How many dead pixels are acceptable?

It depends on the manufacturer’s policy (often based on ISO 9241-307 classes). Check your warranty terms.

Is this dead pixel test free?

Yes. The dead pixel test is free to use in your browser with no account required.
