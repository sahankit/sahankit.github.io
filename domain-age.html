What is domain age?

Domain age usually means how long ago a domain name was first registered. Search engines and buyers sometimes treat older domains as more established, but age alone does not guarantee rankings or trust—you still need good content and links.

This tool reads public registration metadata from RDAP (Registration Data Access Protocol), the modern replacement for classic WHOIS. Results depend on what the registry publishes; some TLDs hide or redact dates.

How to use the checker

Enter a domain like example.com or paste a full URL—we strip paths and optional www. Click Look up to see the registration date, approximate age in years and days, expiration if available, and last-changed date when the registry provides it.

  • Useful for quick SEO research and due diligence before buying a domain.
  • Not a legal record—always confirm with the registrar for contracts.
  • Lookup goes to the public RDAP network; we do not store domains you search.

Frequently asked questions

Is domain age a Google ranking factor?

Google has said domain age alone is not a major direct factor. Helpful content, relevance, and reputation matter more than how old the registration is.

Why does lookup fail for some domains?

Some TLDs block RDAP, rate-limit requests, or omit registration dates. Privacy services may also limit public data.

Do you store the domains I check?

No. The lookup runs from your browser to public RDAP servers; this static site has no backend database for searches.

Is this domain age checker free?

Yes. The domain age checker is free to use in your browser with no account required.

Does my data leave my device?

For most tools on this site, input stays on your device. If a tool calls a public API (for example domain age), that is noted on the page.
