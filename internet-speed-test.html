What your results mean

  • Download (Mbps) — how fast data reaches you: streaming, browsing, downloads. Most plans are sold by this number.
  • Upload (Mbps) — how fast you send data: video calls, uploading files, live streaming.
  • Ping (ms) — the delay for a round trip to the server. Under 30 ms feels instant; under 60 ms is fine for gaming.
  • Jitter (ms) — how much ping varies. Low jitter (under 10 ms) means smooth calls and games.

How much speed do you need?

The test downloads and uploads data over several connections for about eight seconds each, ignoring the first moments while the connection speeds up. For the most accurate result, use a cable or sit near the router, and pause other downloads and streaming.

  • Browsing and email: 3–5 Mbps
  • HD video (1080p) and video calls: 5–10 Mbps per device
  • 4K streaming: 25 Mbps per stream
  • Working from home with a family: 50–100 Mbps or more
  • Online gaming: speed matters less than low ping and jitter

Frequently asked questions

Why is my speed lower than my plan?

Wi-Fi distance and walls, other devices using the connection, older routers and VPNs all reduce speed. Test with a cable next to the router to see your line’s real speed.

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Internet speed is measured in megabits per second (Mbps). Divide by 8 to get megabytes per second: 100 Mbps ≈ 12.5 MB/s.

How much data does the test use?

It depends on your speed: around 20–50 MB on a typical connection and up to a few hundred MB on gigabit fibre.

Is this internet speed test free?

Yes. The internet speed test is free to use in your browser with no account required.
