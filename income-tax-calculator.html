New vs old tax regime in India

From FY 2023–24 onward, the new tax regime under section 115BAC is the default for many salaried employees, but you can still opt for the old regime if it lowers your tax. The new regime offers a higher standard deduction for salary (₹75,000 from FY 2024–25) and simplified slabs, with most Chapter VI-A deductions unavailable. The old regime keeps deductions such as 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh), 80D, HRA exemption, and home loan interest, but uses the classic slab structure and a ₹50,000 standard deduction.

Which regime is advantageous?

There is no single answer: the better regime depends on your gross income and total deductions. Lower deductions (little 80C, no HRA, minimal 80D) often favour the new regime, especially for incomes up to about ₹7–12 lakh where rebates and slabs are favourable. Higher deductions—large HRA in metro cities, full 80C, NPS 80CCD(1B), 80D for family, and housing loan interest—frequently make the old regime cheaper. This calculator compares both side by side for one salary figure so you can see estimated tax, cess, and monthly in-hand.

What this calculator includes

It uses FY 2025–26 slab rates for resident individuals below 60, standard deduction amounts for salaried income, section 87A rebate limits (₹7 lakh total income under the new regime, ₹5 lakh taxable under the old regime), and 4% health and education cess. It does not model surcharge, marginal relief, senior citizen slabs, business income, or capital gains. Verify figures with your employer, CA, or the Income Tax portal before choosing a regime.

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch between new and old regime every year?

Salaried employees can generally choose each financial year when filing or via employer declaration, subject to current IT rules. Business income has stricter lock-in once you leave the old regime.

Is HRA allowed in the new regime?

No. HRA exemption under section 10(13A) is not available in the new tax regime. Enter HRA only for the old-regime column.

Why does the new regime show zero tax below ₹7 lakh?

Section 87A provides a rebate that can reduce tax to nil when total income (after standard deduction) does not exceed ₹7 lakh under the new regime, for eligible residents.
