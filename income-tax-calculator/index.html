New tax regime vs old tax regime — quick comparison

The new regime is the default for many salaried taxpayers but you can opt for the old regime if it reduces your tax. The new regime allows a higher standard deduction on salary (₹75,000) and simpler slabs, but most deductions under Chapter VI-A (including 80C and HRA) are not available. The old regime uses a ₹50,000 standard deduction and the traditional slabs, but you can claim 80C, HRA, 80D, extra NPS, and other eligible deductions.

  • New regime — Often better when you have few deductions (no or low HRA, minimal 80C/80D). Middle incomes may benefit from section 87A rebate when total income after standard deduction is up to ₹7 lakh.
  • Old regime — Often better when HRA exemption is large (metros), you use full 80C, pay health premiums (80D), or have substantial housing loan interest and other VI-A deductions.
  • Which is advantageous? — There is no fixed cutoff salary: run this calculator with your real deduction numbers. A ₹12 lakh package with high HRA may favour the old regime; the same salary with zero HRA may favour the new regime.

FY 2025–26 income tax slab rates (reference)

Slab rates used in this calculator for resident individuals below 60 years:

  • New regime (on taxable income): 0–₹3L nil; ₹3–7L 5%; ₹7–10L 10%; ₹10–12L 15%; ₹12–15L 20%; above ₹15L 30%.
  • Old regime (on taxable income): 0–₹2.5L nil; ₹2.5–5L 5%; ₹5–10L 20%; above ₹10L 30%.
  • Health and education cess: 4% on tax after rebate. Section 87A rebate applied where eligible (new: income up to ₹7L; old: taxable up to ₹5L).

Deductions you can model (old regime only)

For the old regime column, include eligible amounts for 80C/80CCC/80CCD(1) combined (capped at ₹1.5 lakh in this tool), additional NPS under 80CCD(1B) (up to ₹50,000), 80D medical insurance, HRA exemption under section 10(13A), and a lump sum for other VI-A items (80E education loan interest, 80G donations, etc.). Home loan interest under section 24(b) can be added in “other” if you know the annual eligible amount.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better — new tax regime or old tax regime in India?

It depends on your gross income and deductions. Low deductions usually favour the new regime; high HRA, 80C, 80D, and housing interest usually favour the old regime. Use this calculator with your actual numbers to see estimated tax under both.

What is the income tax slab for FY 2025-26 new regime?

This tool uses the post–Budget 2024 style new regime slabs: nil up to ₹3 lakh taxable, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, and 30% on higher bands, plus standard deduction on salary and eligible 87A rebate.

Can I claim 80C in the new tax regime?

No. Most Chapter VI-A deductions including 80C are not available in the new tax regime. Enter 80C only for the old-regime calculation.

Is HRA exemption available in the new tax regime?

No. HRA under section 10(13A) applies only in the old regime. Enter your annual HRA exemption in the old-regime deductions section.

Why is tax zero when salary is around ₹7 lakh in the new regime?

Section 87A can rebate tax to nil for eligible residents when total income (after standard deduction) does not exceed ₹7 lakh under the new regime, subject to current law.

Can salaried employees switch between regimes every year?

Salaried individuals can generally choose the regime each financial year through employer declaration or return filing, subject to current Income Tax rules. Business taxpayers have different lock-in rules.

Does this calculator include 4% cess?

Yes. Health and education cess at 4% is added on tax after section 87A rebate for both regimes.

Is this an official Income Tax Department calculator?

No. This site is independent and not affiliated with the CBDT or Income Tax India. Use official portals for filing and legal advice.

Is this income tax calculator free?

Yes. The income tax calculator is free to use in your browser with no account required.
